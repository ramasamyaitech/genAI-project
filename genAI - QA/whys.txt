If you say **"I built a RAG application using FAISS, GPT-5/T5, LangChain, and embeddings"**, the interviewer will usually ask **"Why?"** questions to check whether you truly understand the design decisions.

Here are the most common follow-up questions with simple interview answers.

---

# 1. Why did you use RAG instead of fine-tuning?

**Answer:**
"RAG allows the model to answer using the latest documents without retraining the model. Fine-tuning is expensive, time-consuming, and updates require retraining, whereas RAG only needs the vector database to be updated."

---

# 2. Why did you choose FAISS?

**Answer:**
"FAISS provides very fast similarity search over millions of vectors. It's open source, easy to integrate, and performs efficient nearest-neighbor searches."

---

# 3. Why not store documents directly in SQL?

**Answer:**
"SQL databases search exact text or keywords. RAG requires semantic search based on meaning, which vector databases like FAISS support."

---

# 4. Why do we convert text into embeddings?

**Answer:**
"LLMs cannot compare document meaning directly. Embeddings convert text into numerical vectors so semantically similar content is close together in vector space."

---

# 5. Why do we split documents into chunks?

**Answer:**
"Large documents exceed the model's context window. Chunking helps retrieve only the relevant sections and improves retrieval accuracy."

---

# 6. Why use chunk overlap?

**Answer:**
"Important information may span two chunks. Overlap preserves context and reduces information loss."

Example:

```
Chunk 1:
The customer signed the...

Chunk 2:
...agreement on Monday.
```

Without overlap, the sentence is split awkwardly.

---

# 7. Why did you choose 500–1000 character chunks?

**Answer:**
"It's a good balance. Very small chunks lose context, while very large chunks include unnecessary information and reduce retrieval quality."

---

# 8. Why use cosine similarity?

**Answer:**
"Cosine similarity measures how similar two vectors are based on their direction rather than magnitude, making it suitable for semantic search."

---

# 9. Why Top-K = 3 or 5?

**Answer:**
"One document may not contain the complete answer. Retrieving the top 3–5 relevant chunks usually provides enough context without overwhelming the model."

---

# 10. Why not Top-K = 20?

**Answer:**
"Too many retrieved chunks increase token usage, latency, cost, and can introduce irrelevant information."

---

# 11. Why GPT-5?

**Answer:**
"GPT-5 provides strong reasoning, instruction following, and natural language generation, making it suitable for answering questions from retrieved context."

---

# 12. Why T5?

**Answer:**
"T5 is useful for summarization, question answering, and text transformation tasks. It can also be self-hosted."

---

# 13. Why not use only GPT?

**Answer:**
"GPT doesn't know private company documents unless they're provided. RAG retrieves relevant documents first, then GPT answers using that context."

---

# 14. Why LangChain?

**Answer:**
"LangChain simplifies connecting document loaders, chunking, embeddings, vector stores, retrievers, and LLMs into one workflow."

---

# 15. Why prompt engineering?

**Answer:**
"Good prompts reduce hallucinations and guide the model to answer only from the provided context."

---

# 16. Why did hallucinations happen?

**Answer:**
"If the retrieved context is incomplete or irrelevant, the LLM may generate a plausible but incorrect answer."

---

# 17. How did you reduce hallucinations?

**Answer:**

* Better retrieval
* Better embeddings
* Improved prompts
* Ask the model to say **'I don't know'** when information is missing
* Use reranking

---

# 18. Why cache embeddings?

**Answer:**
"Generating embeddings is computationally expensive. Caching avoids recomputing them for unchanged documents."

---

# 19. Why cache GPT responses?

**Answer:**
"It reduces latency and API costs for repeated questions."

---

# 20. Why save the FAISS index?

**Answer:**
"Rebuilding the index every time is slow. Saving and loading the index allows faster application startup."

---

# 21. Why metadata filtering?

**Answer:**
"It limits retrieval to relevant documents, such as a specific department, project, or date range."

---

# 22. Why reranking?

**Answer:**
"FAISS retrieves approximate nearest neighbors. A reranker can reorder the results so the most relevant chunks are sent to the LLM."

---

# 23. Why not send the entire PDF?

**Answer:**
"Large PDFs exceed token limits, increase costs, and include irrelevant information. Retrieving only relevant chunks is more efficient."

---

# 24. Why vector search instead of keyword search?

**Answer:**
"Vector search understands meaning, while keyword search only matches exact words."

Example:

```
Question:
How do I reset my password?

Document:
Change login credentials.
```

Keyword search may fail because "password" isn't present, while vector search recognizes the semantic similarity.

---

# 25. What happens if the user uploads a new document?

**Answer:**
"The document is loaded, chunked, converted into embeddings, and added to the FAISS index so it becomes searchable."

---

# 26. Why use OCR?

**Answer:**
"Scanned PDFs contain images instead of selectable text. OCR extracts text so it can be embedded and searched."

---

# 27. Why not fine-tune every time documents change?

**Answer:**
"Fine-tuning is slow and expensive. RAG allows new knowledge to be added immediately by updating the vector database."

---

# 28. Why monitor latency?

**Answer:**
"Users expect quick responses. We monitor retrieval time, embedding time, LLM response time, and total request latency."

---

## One Real-Time Scenario an Interviewer May Ask

**Q:** *A user asks a question, but the correct answer exists in the PDF and your RAG still returns a wrong answer. What would you check?*

**Answer:**

1. Verify the text was extracted correctly from the PDF.
2. Check chunk size and overlap.
3. Confirm embeddings were generated successfully.
4. Ensure the FAISS index contains the new vectors.
5. Inspect the retrieved Top-K chunks.
6. Improve retrieval (Top-K, metadata filters, reranking).
7. Review the prompt to ensure the LLM answers only from the retrieved context.

These "why" and troubleshooting questions are among the most common follow-ups in interviews for RAG, LangChain, and LLM application development.
